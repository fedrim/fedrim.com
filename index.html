Why FedRIM

Mapping the rim

Federal research and innovation, mapped for the space age.

In space, the outer rim is the far edge of the galaxy: past the settled worlds, where the maps run out and new stars are still forming. As we head into the space age, federal research has a rim of its own, and it keeps moving outward: the government labs, and the research institutions and companies they work with, building what does not exist yet.

Much of that work leaves a public record: patent filings, ownership records, grants and contracts. But the records are spread across agencies and databases, and they are hard to see whole. FedRIM, Federal Research and Innovation Mapped, puts them on one map, so anyone can see where federal research is pushing outward, who it reaches, and where the money goes.

It starts with the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs: 1,762 patent records that list VA as an owner, the 76 VA medical centers they are tied to, the 86 universities, hospitals, companies and other organizations that own them with VA, and VA's money in Funded by You. More agencies are coming.

Method

About the records

USPTO assignment records read Sept 28–29, 2026, and patent records read to Nov 16, 2026. Assignments recorded since then come in the next release.

01What the map shows

U.S. granted patents and published patent applications whose USPTO assignment records list the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs as an owner: 1,762 records (1,106 granted patents and 656 published applications). Records where VA released its interest or holds only a license, provisional applications and unpublished application numbers are left out. 1,745 of the records are on the map, as a line from a VA site to each partner, or as a loop beside the VA site when there is no partner; 178 also have a line to a second VA site that public records connect their inventors to. The other 17 are in the patent finder only.

02Where a record is drawn from

Each record is drawn from the VA site that public records connect to its inventors (their VA affiliation in PubMed, NIH RePORTER and ClinicalTrials.gov records). Inventors are matched to those records by name, so a few matches may be wrong. Some records are drawn instead from the VA site nearest their partner organization or their inventors' cities; those are marked inferred. The inventors are not named.

03What USPTO ownership records show

USPTO assignment records show who has recorded an ownership interest, and when it was recorded. They do not show licenses or agreements between owners, and some interests are recorded late or never. An organization can hold rights that do not appear here, and a recorded owner may have agreements that change who controls a patent.

04People

The map names no inventors. Individuals who are recorded owners are counted, not named.

05Where the records come from

Every assignment record naming VA in the USPTO Assignment Center, read Sept 28–29, 2026 (assignments recorded through Sept 25, 2026), and the patent records read to Nov 16, 2026, with each record's text and status from Google Patents. Inferred sites use the inventors' cities from USPTO's Open Data Portal. Assignments recorded since then come in the next release.
